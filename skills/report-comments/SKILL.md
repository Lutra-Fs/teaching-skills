# Report Comments

Turn the teacher's judgement into comments a parent can read and act on. The teacher supplies the grade and the evidence; the skill drafts the wording, and every draft goes back to the teacher.

## Inputs

1. The school's rules: word or character limit, whether comments are per subject or general, any required structure. School rules outrank the defaults below.
2. The grade scale, fetched rather than recalled: the NESA Common Grade Scale for Years 7-10, the course performance descriptors at the end of Stage 5, or the Common Grade Scale for Preliminary courses in Year 11. Record the source.
3. Per student: initials or a placeholder, the teacher's grade, two or three observations from the reporting period (what they can do, one thing to work on), and the course content the comment refers to. Real names go in only in the school's reporting system.

Ask once for whatever is missing. The grade is the teacher's; leave a student without one unwritten and say so.

## Writing rules (NSW Department of Education guidance)

- Addressed to the parent or carer, in the third person, using the student's official given name (as a placeholder here).
- Starts with a specific strength tied to what the student knows and can do in this course.
- Gives at least one area for improvement **and how it can be achieved**.
- Plain English: no syllabus codes or jargon. "Could" or "may" for what comes next, never "will" or "should".
- Behaviour only where it bears on learning (strengths, areas to develop, commitment to learning). No first-person sign-off.
- The wording matches the grade: the comment says what the student did at the level the scale describes, without restating the grade unless the school asks.
- Each comment rests on that student's evidence. Comments for students with similar observations still differ in what they name, so no two read as the same comment with a name swapped.

## Steps

1. Collect the inputs above; lock the scale wording.
2. Draft each comment to the limit.
3. Check the set: every comment has a strength, an improvement and how; no comment contains a claim the teacher did not supply; no two open the same way or share a sentence; every comment is within the limit; no names other than placeholders.
4. Deliver each comment in its own copyable block, with the checks' result and any students left unwritten.

Done when every student with a grade has a draft that passes step 3.

## AI use in NSW public schools

The department endorses NSWEduChat for this kind of work and does not endorse other free AI tools; with any other tool, use placeholders only and follow the department's minimum safety practices. The teacher's professional judgement stays final.
