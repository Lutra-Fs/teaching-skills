# Assessment and reporting

Read for any assessment plan, task, rubric, feedback or data process, or reporting request.

## Authority first

Verify the exact course and cohort and the school-adopted syllabus; the faculty schedule, common task and its owner; the NESA course assessment page, ACE rules, course specifications and school policy; the required task type, components, weightings, timing, notification and conditions; and the adjustment, malpractice, authorship, GenAI and appeals procedures. Sources: [Assessment and reporting](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/assessment-and-reporting), [Assessment principles](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/assessment-and-reporting/assessment-principles), [Assessment in Stage 6](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/assessment-and-reporting/assessment-in-stage-6), and the course's Assessment tab.

Work on a faculty common task stays inside its authorised boundaries and keeps cross-class comparability; material changes go to the task owner or head of faculty as proposals.

## Pick the layer

Name the layer the request needs before producing anything: whole-course program or schedule; one task, its notification and criteria; marking and moderation; feedback and student response; evidence analysis, reporting and next teaching; or post-task evaluation. Connect it to the layers before and after it.

## Backward-map the task

`outcome and content → target thinking → observable evidence → criteria and marking guidance → feedback and reporting → next teaching`

The task must elicit the required knowledge or skill itself, beyond the topic. Define: purpose (diagnostic, formative, self or peer, summative); target thinking and irrelevant barriers; taught content and selected outcomes; product or performance, and an authentic context where useful; conditions, time, milestones and permitted support; the evidence state where it affects the inference; how evidence is recorded; how students use feedback; moderation; privacy and the next instruction.

## Quality check

- **Validity:** task, evidence and criteria match the target thinking and outcomes.
- **Reliability:** instructions, marking guidance, exemplars and moderation support consistent judgement.
- **Equity and access:** students can show the target thinking without irrelevant barriers; confirmed adjustments are available.
- **Transparency:** students understand purpose, conditions and quality.
- **Workload and timing:** timing supports learning, and marking and feedback are manageable.
- **Objectivity:** criteria focus on relevant evidence.
- **Feedback use:** students have protected time to act on feedback, and the task plans how they use it: a rewrite of the response, or a similar task that applies the same point. Where a grade would stop the reading, the feedback reaches students first and the grade after they have responded to it.

## Rubrics and marking guidance

Write criteria with observable performance descriptions, drawing language from outcomes, content, standards, band descriptions and the task's marking guidance.

- Write the top performance first.
- Progress through depth, accuracy, complexity, control and independence; each level describes what the work shows.
- Keep criteria distinct and proportionate to the target thinking.
- List acceptable alternative evidence or solutions.
- Test: another teacher reaches a similar judgement from the task, rubric and work sample alone.

### Feedback delivery

Individual written comments are one mode, chosen when the task warrants them. Three others carry the same points at lower cost: whole-class verbal feedback on the patterns the work shows; coded annotations, where the codes are taught to the class before first use and suit students who can read a code without the sentence beside it; and in-the-moment feedback while circulating. The plan names the mode and what each mode asks students to do next.

## Exemplars and audits of teaching artifacts

- **Exemplar:** start from the curriculum, task constraints, audience and criteria, and make it show the target thinking. Scaffold from naming and describing toward explaining, analysing and evaluating, with prompts fading as independence grows.
- **Calibrate to the course before judging fit:** read the syllabus support document (key terms and its "not required" lines) beside the dot points, check the artifact's claimed cohort against it, and separate required, optional and excluded content for this task. Method and worked case: `courses/maths-standard-advanced.md`.
- **Generated lesson, task or answer key:** recompute every value in the key (model-written keys carry plausible slips such as three-decimal money or mismatched exponents). Tag each finding as marker-deductible or a calibration note, and give one verdict: approve, request named changes, or escalate a school decision.
- **"Is this too much?"** Classify the excess in one line each: lesson pacing (quantify the overshoot in minutes), teacher-side paper (usually harmless when it costs no class time), or coverage bought by leaving content out.

## Reporting routes

- **Years 7–10:** the Common Grade Scale, as holistic, on-balance judgements.
- **End of Stage 5:** the course's Course Performance Descriptors where they apply.
- **Year 11:** the Common Grade Scale for Preliminary courses; Life Skills and VET follow `stage-and-pathway.md`.
- **Year 12:** the course's HSC Performance Band Descriptions, Achievement Level Descriptions, submitted mark or other route.

A reporting scale describes a Stage or course; lesson tasks get their own criteria.

## Stage 6 formal assessment

Before drafting, capture: the exact Year 11 or 12 course and syllabus version; mandatory versus sample program elements; components and weightings; constraints on number, type and weighting, and prescribed tasks; the school schedule and task number; content already taught; notification lead time and required fields; examination, project or submitted-work specifications; moderation, mark recording, malpractice, authorship and appeals. Weightings and task rules come from this course's own documents.

For a common or summative task, verify the approved conditions and adjustments before including prompts, templates, checkpoints or teacher assistance, and record any support that supplied a criterion-relevant decision.

## HSC exam evidence

Past and sample HSC materials show examined demand; school assessment rules come from the school and NESA assessment pages. For HSC papers, online exams, marking guidelines, marker feedback or standards material, read `hsc-exam-intelligence.md` and map each insight back to full syllabus coverage and the school program.
