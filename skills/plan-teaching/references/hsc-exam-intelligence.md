# HSC exam intelligence

Use this reference for HSC past/sample examinations, online exams, marking guidelines, marker feedback, standards samples or exam-demand mapping. Use exam evidence to improve coverage, progression, practice and feedback; the syllabus still defines what is taught.

## Applicability gate

Lock:

- exact course, Year 12 pathway and school-adopted syllabus;
- cohort, calendar year and relevant exam year;
- assessment/exam specifications and course-specific project or submitted-work requirements;
- source type: sample exam, actual past exam, online exam, marking guideline, marker feedback or standards sample;
- compatibility with the target course/version.

Use:

- `compatible`: same course and applicable syllabus/specification;
- `partial_mapping_required`: an older/outgoing paper contains individually mappable demand;
- `sample_only`: official sample for a course without an actual exam under that syllabus;
- `online_only`: official exam experience exists but no downloadable paper;
- `incompatible` or `unverified`: do not use for a teaching decision without further evidence.

## Evidence chain

Use the sources in this order:

`syllabus/content -> assessment and exam specifications -> sample exam -> compatible past exam -> marking guidelines -> marker feedback -> standards samples/performance descriptions -> teaching decision`

- Syllabus/content defines coverage.
- Specifications define current examination form and tools.
- Papers reveal realised demand for that year.
- Marking guidelines reveal criteria and acceptable response scope; any included sample answer is not automatically exemplary or complete.
- Marker feedback identifies reported strengths and areas for improvement.
- Standards materials illustrate performance at stated band boundaries.

Aggregate mapped questions by demand, not only topic or mark frequency. Distinguish knowledge recall, procedure, application, reasoning, integration, communication, practical/project process and use of stimulus or digital tools.

## Course-sensitive guardrails

- For a replacement syllabus before its first HSC exam, use the official sample materials and map older questions individually, labelled with their own syllabus.
- When NESA provides an online exam with no downloadable paper, record the official access route, familiarisation requirements, marking guidance and feedback as the source.
- For Design and Technology Stage 6, verify and plan for both the written examination and Major Design Project/folio requirements. Written-paper history alone cannot represent the course.
- Keep Enterprise Computing and Software Engineering specifications, tools and question demands separate.
- Life Skills has no HSC examination. VET optional examinations follow the exact VET and RTO authority route.

## Translate evidence into teaching

Produce only what the request needs:

- compatibility/source ledger;
- question-demand and common-error map;
- whole-course coverage gap or overemphasis check;
- response-construction, tool-familiarity or timed-practice progression;
- retrieval/interleaving and feedback plan;
- unit/term insertion points and lost-time recovery;
- next evidence/review point.

Limits on what exam evidence supports:

- Frequency weights revision time. It forecasts no specific question, and rare or unexamined syllabus content is still taught.
- A question structure reused in a school task is checked for validity, security, copyright and school ownership.
- School task count, weightings and schedule come from the school's assessment program, never from the external exam.
- The output is the analysis asked for; a mock exam, unit or lesson follows when requested.

Primary routes:

- [HSC exam papers](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers)
- [HSC standards materials](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-standards)
- exact course Assessment/HSC examination page and current record of changes
