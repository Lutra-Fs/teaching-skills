# Scaffolding, fading and differentiation

Read for a scaffolding, fading or differentiation decision: worked examples, prompts and templates, tiered support, productive struggle, or a review of whether students depend on support. **Target thinking** is the decision or performance students must ultimately make themselves (select a strategy, formulate a problem, debug, justify, evaluate a design); every support decision protects it.

## Terms

| Term | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| Scaffolding | Temporary support, calibrated to current evidence, that lets students take part in target thinking they cannot yet do alone. A resource becomes a scaffold through how it is used and adapted. |
| Fading | Evidence-triggered transfer of responsibility to students; support may reduce, change form or be restored. |
| Differentiation | Varying access, process, support, complexity or response from learner evidence while the goal stays the same. |
| Reasonable adjustment | A confirmed access measure from the school's process; it stays until that process changes it. |
| Gradual release | An architecture (model, guide, release) that can carry scaffolding toward independence. |

A whole-class worked example can scaffold without differentiating; a response-mode adjustment can differentiate without being temporary. Authentic tools (calculator, IDE, reference sheet, assistive technology) may remain in independent performance; classify them as tools or adjustments.

## Procedure

1. **Name the target thinking.**
2. **Read current evidence:** prerequisite check, student work, observation, hinge response or prior artifact, with its limits. One attempt shows one attempt.
3. **Locate the barrier:** prerequisite knowledge, language or representation, working memory and task organisation, strategy, self-regulation, participation, or safety competence.
4. **Choose sufficient support.** For unfamiliar content with missing prerequisites, start with explanation and modelling; for a gap in otherwise established learning, a light cue may be enough. Remove the irrelevant barrier and keep the target thinking with students.
5. **Set the contingency before teaching:** which response means continue, strengthen, partly model, reteach, regroup, extend or withdraw. A useful ladder, moved up or down from the response: open prompt, strategic cue, narrowed choice, partial completion, model one critical step, explicit reteach.
6. **Fade from evidence:** accurate explanation, fewer errors, apt strategy choice, self-correction, success in a less familiar context. Fading can run from full model to partial model to cue to self-prompt, from supplied to student-generated questions, or from a familiar to a transfer case. When performance drops, restore or redesign the support.
7. **Record the evidence state** for the handoff: `modelled | guided | prompted | independent | transfer`. These are provenance labels, not an attainment scale.

**Done when** each support names its barrier, the target thinking it protects, the response that changes it, and how independent or transfer performance will be checked.

Support that stays in force throughout: WHS controls, supervision, PPE and stop or check procedures; authorised assessment conditions and confirmed adjustments (changed only through their own process); curriculum, authorship, ethics, privacy and safeguarding requirements.

## Hard and soft scaffolds

Hard (resource) scaffolds are worked examples, completion problems, checklists, diagrams, starter code, templates and prompts. They earn their place when they target a known barrier, expose disciplinary thinking or free the teacher to respond. Soft (interactive) scaffolds are questioning, prompting, feedback, partial modelling, think-aloud and conferencing. For each hard scaffold, ask: which barrier, which decisions stay with students, what response changes it, and what later evidence shows independent use. When several resources stack (rubric, checklist, template, organiser), give each a distinct function or cut it.

## By subject

- **Mathematics:** for procedural fluency, worked examples and completion problems keep the intended thinking. For modelling or problem formulation, scaffold entry and monitoring and leave the choice of variables, representation, assumptions and method visible to students. Fade toward an independent, then a varied problem, judged by errors and explanations as well as answers.
- **Computing:** where syntax is an access barrier, starter code or an API reminder fits. Where algorithm design or debugging is the target, use test output, trace prompts, narrowed search areas or partial cases, and fade toward student-written tests and debugging plans. Keep individual authorship evidence inside group work.
- **TAS design and practical work:** exemplars, process headings, demonstrations and checkpoints expose quality and process; decisions about user need, criteria, ideation, trade-offs, testing and evaluation stay with students. A safety demonstration can enable practical independence while the safety system stays in force. In a Design and Technology Major Design Project, support project management and reflection; the design decisions and folio are the student's.
- **Stage 6:** teach response architecture and standards during learning, then move toward authentic independent conditions. For extended writing, scaffold along the verb progression identify, describe, explain, analyse, evaluate (with ALARM where the school uses it), taking each verb's meaning from the NESA glossary, and fade the prompts. Past-paper patterns weight revision time across the whole syllabus.

## Assessment and differentiation

- **Formative work:** dynamic support is expected; record its level so the evidence reads correctly.
- **Summative or common tasks:** use the approved conditions and adjustments only. A scaffold that supplies a criterion-relevant decision changes what the result can show; record it.
- **Tiering:** when class evidence shows a spread, keep one learning intention with support, core and stretch entry points that differ in scaffolding, complexity or independence. Group flexibly from current evidence and name tiers by the task. To produce the versions as files, follow `derived-resources.md`. Life Skills, prior-stage access, reasonable adjustment and ordinary differentiation are four different routes; name the one that applies.
- **Productive struggle** is a design choice inside adequate instruction, access and safety support.
