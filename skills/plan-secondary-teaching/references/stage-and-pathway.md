# Stage, pathway and scope routing

Use this reference for Stage emphasis, Life Skills, VET and requests outside the supported Mathematics/TAS scope.

## Scope boundary

Deep support is limited to Mathematics and the TAS courses with their own card in `courses/` (see `courses/tas-common.md`).

- Another TAS course is `specialist-unchecked`: draft from its locked syllabus and leave specialist safety controls to the school. Another KLA is `out_of_scope`.
- For an interdisciplinary task, plan only the supported Mathematics/TAS lane and preserve any supplied partner-subject constraints. Require a subject specialist to confirm the other lane.
- Food Technology is a `specialist-unchecked` route governed by `courses/tas-other.md`, not a generic other-subject route.
- VET is a separate authority workflow, not a variant of the generic assessment pack.

## Stage/cohort emphasis

Apply these priorities inside the adopted course plan, as prompts for judgement. Every Stage has course obligations and needs learning evidence; `continuity-progression.md` handles conflicts between recovery time, coverage and fixed milestones.

### Stage 4

- Diagnose transition knowledge and routines.
- Make disciplinary vocabulary, processes and quality visible.
- Consider chunk length, explicit demonstration, entry points and scaffold fading from actual learner evidence.
- Establish safe participation and resource routines.

### Stage 5

- Inspect readiness for deeper reasoning, authentic/extended work, project management and increasing independence.
- Increase independence only when evidence supports it.
- Use applicable course performance standards for holistic end-of-Stage reporting, not as a rubric for every lesson.
- Maintain course coverage and foundations for relevant Stage 6 pathways without narrowing the curriculum to exam preparation.

### Stage 6

- Resolve Year 11/12, exact course/type, syllabus version and target term.
- Maintain required outcomes/content, components, weightings, course specifications and school assessment schedule.
- Track intended/taught/evidenced coverage, lessons remaining, disruptions, revision and recovery.
- Include retrieval, response construction, standards/exam familiarity and timed practice where relevant.
- Retain differentiation, adjustments, conceptual depth and feedback; content counts as learned when students show it.

Stage-specific language rules for slides and student text live in `workflow-lesson.md` (section 3).

## Mainstream and Life Skills

Life Skills is a course decision made through collaborative curriculum planning and recorded by the school; a student who is behind, disengaged or has health or behaviour needs gets ordinary adjustments and differentiation in the mainstream course. Activate Life Skills only from that recorded decision. Then:

- verify eligibility, the principal-led decision/certification process and the required student/parent/carer collaboration;
- use the selected Life Skills outcomes/content;
- keep the route distinct from mainstream outcomes in the same subject as required;
- design opportunities to demonstrate selected outcomes in suitable contexts;
- follow current NESA assessment, reporting, entry, completion and credentialling rules (Life Skills has no A–E or formal-assessment assumptions);
- record achievement independently or with support where required.

For Stage 6 Life Skills, NESA does not require formal assessment or A–E reporting, and there is no HSC examination. Indicative hours and satisfactory completion requirements still apply. Recheck [NESA Life Skills in Stage 6](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/ace-rules/ace11/life-skills-s6).

## VET

Classify VET as `out_of_scope_authority_handoff`: competency assessment belongs to the RTO's own system.

Require:

- the current Training Package/qualification and units of competency;
- an RTO with the qualification/units on scope;
- the RTO training and assessment strategy;
- a qualified assessor, evidence rules, validation and recordkeeping;
- NESA credentialling and optional HSC examination status where applicable;
- work placement, WHS and industry requirements.

The optional Framework HSC examination is separate from competency assessment, which keeps its own evidence rules.

## Course type

For Stage 6, distinguish Board Developed, Content Endorsed and Board Endorsed courses, and check whether this course has:

- an HSC examination;
- a submitted school-based assessment mark;
- the same grade/performance scale;
- the same component weightings;
- a mandatory project or practical examination.

Read the exact course overview, assessment page, specifications and ACE rules.

## Differentiation across Stages

Use a common learning goal where appropriate and vary:

- access and representation;
- scaffold/prompt level;
- examples and context;
- grouping and support;
- complexity, depth and independence;
- product mode only when it preserves the construct.

Consider tiering a task when the class evidence shows a spread: one learning intention, with support, core and stretch entry points that differ in scaffolding, complexity or independence. Tiering and the four support routes are set out in `scaffolding-differentiation.md`; follow the school or university template where it fixes the format.
