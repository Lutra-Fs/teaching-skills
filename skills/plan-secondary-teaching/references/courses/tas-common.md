# TAS common guidance

Read when the TAS course is still unresolved, or when designing a project, practical activity or assessment for any TAS course. TAS is not one interchangeable subject: resolve the exact course first, starting from [NESA TAS syllabuses](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/tas).

## Support levels

| Level | Courses | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| deep | Technology 7–8; Computing Technology 7–10; Design and Technology 7–10 or Design Innovation Technology 7–10; Design and Technology Stage 6; Enterprise Computing 11–12; Software Engineering 11–12 | Full lock and course card, design within the school program |
| specialist-unchecked | Every other TAS course | `tas-other.md` |
| out of scope | Other KLAs; VET competency assessment | Plan the Mathematics or TAS contribution and name the specialist or RTO for the rest |

A technology context does not identify the course: Technology 7–8 focus areas are not electives, and "D&T" or "timber" needs resolving. See the course cards.

## Common design

Where the syllabus calls for it: an authentic brief, user and criteria; systems, computational or design thinking; iterative definition, planning, production, testing and evaluation; product and process evidence; individual and collaborative evidence; subject-specific documentation; safe, secure and ethical practice. A polished product must not hide missing process evidence or understanding.

## Activity and project design

Specify the learning and observable output; the outcome and evidence; student-facing instructions; a demonstration before distribution where needed; roles, resource caps, checkpoints and the visible next step; teacher questions and likely failures; evidence-triggered support and stretch that leave the disciplinary decisions with students; setup, transitions, safety and pack-up.

Design, debugging, user and criteria decisions, trade-offs, testing and evaluation are usually the target thinking, so they stay with students (`../scaffolding-differentiation.md`). Mandatory safety controls stay in force at every stage.

## Runtime checks by lesson type

Use the set that matches the lesson type.

- **Software, data or network:** the course's own card where it lists them (Software Engineering, Enterprise Computing, Computing Technology 7–10); for any other course: accounts, devices and connectivity; starter files and expected output; storage and backup; privacy and safe practice; a debugging checkpoint.
- **Design or project:** brief, user and context; research and ideation evidence; criteria and constraints; roles, milestones and decision log; prototype, test and iteration; individual contribution; evaluation and next iteration.
- **Workshop or material production:** teacher and student tool competence; PPE, guards, exclusion zones and supervision; bookings and quantities; demonstration and stop/check points; safe waiting task; product and process evidence; shutdown, inventory and cleanup.
- **Food or agricultural practical:** hygiene, allergen and contamination controls; equipment and shared stations; sequence, timing and teacher visibility; waste; product, technique and process evidence; cleanup and storage.

## Assessment evidence

Balance as the construct requires: knowledge and understanding, practical application, design or computational thinking, process documentation, product quality, testing and iteration, communication and collaboration. Not every TAS task is a project, and not every Computing task is code.
