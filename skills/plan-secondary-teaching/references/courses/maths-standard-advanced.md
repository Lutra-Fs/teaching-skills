# Standard versus Advanced mathematics calibration

Use this guide when a lesson, task or exemplar must be pitched to one mathematics
course and checked against another (Standard 1, Standard 2, Advanced), or audited
for cohort fit. Lock the adopted syllabus, cohort and version first.

## Locate the relevant evidence

Read the adopted syllabus together with its matching support document. Extract
the exact course, year and topic section; Standard 1 and Standard 2 can have
separate teaching advice. Keywords and term counts help locate passages, but
cannot establish which course requires, permits or excludes an activity.

Read key terms, content, teaching advice and prior knowledge in context. A key
terms list is not automatically a closed vocabulary list. Keep these findings
separate:

- Required content or assessable expectations, with their course/topic scope.
- Not required: no expectation to teach or assess it follows from that clause;
  this alone does not prohibit enrichment.
- Suggested or permitted enrichment: retain the source's conditions and learner
  scope. Decide its usefulness against the current lesson goals.
- Explicit restrictions: apply only what the source actually excludes, without
  inventing mention limits or extending a topic-specific clause to the course.

Return a compact comparison: planned term/activity → source section and wording
→ expectation or uncertainty → implication for this lesson. Record version and
cohort. Where the source leaves applicability open, record the gap as open; a
design is a curriculum violation only where the source excludes it.

## Historical 2024 syllabus example

The 2026-09-04 comparison used local 2024 Stage 6 Standard and Advanced syllabus
and support DOCX files.
These are locating examples, not a current curriculum lock.

The Standard support section “Teaching advice for Algebraic relationships
(Year 12 – Standard 2)” says students are not required to factorise or use the
quadratic formula **when graphing quadratic relationships**, and encourages a
table of values. Its graph-intercept advice separates reading the graph from
finding intercepts using an equation. Contextual restrictions do not require
formal domain/range notation. The exponential/reciprocal graphing advice notes
that some students may benefit from understanding asymptotes without expecting
that extension from everyone.

Those scoped passages support distinguishing core expectations from enrichment.
They do not establish a ban on enrichment, a maximum number of mentions, or a
course-wide closed vocabulary. Verify the current adopted passages before
applying them to a new task, and cite the matching support when explaining a
choice of graphing method, contextual language or differentiation.
