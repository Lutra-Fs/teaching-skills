# External resources and AI-generated texts

Read when scouting supporting material for a new lesson, when selecting or materially changing a platform, textbook or external resource (Mathspace, Digital Technologies Hub and similar), or when students will read AI-generated explanations, code documentation or interactive material. Reuse existing alignment and access records for unchanged resources.

## Scouting supporting material

For a lesson taught for the first time, or when the teacher asks, go and look for material that connects the core concept to students' own lives. Scout before writing the blueprint, show the teacher the candidates, and wait: the teacher decides which to use, or none, and the blueprint is written around that choice. In a run with nobody to answer, follow `workflow-lesson.md` section 1 step 5.

| Stage | Look first for | How students use it |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | A short animated or explainer video; an everyday object, app or game they already use | Watch a named segment, then answer literal questions before any inference |
| 5 | A short explainer video or a current news item about a product or event they know | Watch or read with guiding questions that move from describe to explain |
| 6 | A real organisation's own writing: an engineering or company blog post, an incident write-up, product documentation, a regulator's case note | Reading comprehension with NESA glossary verbs, or a research task (one search phrase and one or two checked links, as in `workflow-lesson.md`) |

- Bring back two or three **candidates** for each kind that fits. For each: the link, what it shows, where it would sit in the lesson, and which planned segment it **replaces**. For a video add the full length and the start and end times to play; for a text add its length and reading level.
- Search Australian and current first: NSW Department of Education and NESA resources, ABC Education, the eSafety Commissioner, the Digital Technologies Hub, and OAIC or ACSC case material. Elsewhere, weigh who published it and why.
- A Stage 6 text from an organisation carries that organisation's interest. Build the first question on source and purpose, using the Text-Critic check below.
- Prefer material in which this class can see its own backgrounds and experiences, and whose setting the class already knows. Leave out a candidate that leans on context the class lacks and the lesson does not teach (What Works Best 2025: Classroom management and Assessment guides).
- When nothing suitable turns up for a kind, say so and move on.
- A chosen candidate then passes the gate below, has its facts checked and dated, and has its full time counted in the period.

**Done when** each kind that fits the lesson has candidates or a stated "none found", every candidate names the segment it replaces, and the teacher has answered, or the choice is listed as an assumption in an unattended run.

## Computing disciplinary literacy for AI-generated and interactive texts

Computing students increasingly read AI explanations, generated code documentation, error analyses, visualisations, and other non-linear or interactive material. Extend a reading routine beyond highlighting or static annotation when the medium requires it. A Text-Critic check can ask students to identify the source and purpose, test whether the explanation fits the local code or data, inspect assumptions and missing context, verify an output with a small independent check, and record what remains uncertain. Preserve the other reading purposes when useful: locating information, building meaning, and using the text to solve a task are not replaced by criticism.

Treat AI output as material to evaluate, not as automatically false or authoritative. Choose the verification step from the course task and the disciplinary risk. The routine should reveal whether students can transfer the check to a new explanation, code fragment, dataset, or interface rather than only annotate a supplied static passage.

## External resource gate

For a newly selected or materially changed platform/textbook/resource, check the relevant fields below. Reuse established alignment and access records for unchanged resources; expose only adaptations and unresolved issues:

1. Purpose: model, guided practice, independent practice, retrieval, diagnosis, assessment, stretch or project stimulus.
2. Exact NESA/school alignment and any mismatch.
3. Disciplinary/cognitive demand and evidence revealed.
4. Accessibility, scaffold purpose, contingency/fading and construct preservation.
5. Time, devices/materials, safety, teacher visibility and contingency.
6. Licensing, attribution, privacy/data use and school approval.
7. Adaptations and the actual student-facing instructions.

The approved program sets the sequence; a textbook or platform sequence is mapped onto it.

### Mathspace

Use [Mathspace Australia](https://mathspace.co/au/) selectively for Mathematics worked examples/solutions, practice, adaptive support, diagnostic checks, worksheets, tests and step-level data.

Before use:

- select the exact curriculum/textbook edition and course;
- remap to the verified NESA/school program;
- inspect method, representation, difficulty, hints and misconception evidence;
- corroborate analytics with student reasoning/work;
- check licence, accounts, accessibility, privacy and device availability;
- provide a non-platform alternative.

### Digital Learning Selector

The NSW Department of Education's [Digital Learning Selector](https://app.education.nsw.gov.au/digital-learning-selector) lists 50+ activities that embed ICT in a lesson (for example design thinking, virtual manipulatives, daily check-ins), filtered by Bloom's level, each with a short description and free Google or Microsoft templates. Use it for activity formats, not content; the lesson's core still comes from the program and syllabus.

### Digital Technologies Hub

Use the Australian Government Department of Education's [Digital Technologies Hub](https://www.digitaltechnologieshub.edu.au/) for Technology 7–8 Digital and communication technologies or Computing Technology 7–10 scope-and-sequence ideas, lesson resources, unplugged options, inclusive resources, technology choices, programming pedagogy and assessment examples.

Its resources are principally organised around the Australian Curriculum, and the Hub directs teachers to jurisdiction/sector requirements. Remap every selected idea to the current NSW course outcome/content and school context. Cite NSW NESA codes; Australian Curriculum codes are a different system.
