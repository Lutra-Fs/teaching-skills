# Teaching decisions and framework audits

Read when designing a new task or unit from scratch, or when the teacher asks for a framework audit (UbD, QTM, WWB, UDL, APST). Apply the principles while designing; name a framework only when it explains a consequential choice or the teacher asked. NESA and the school program set the requirements; these frameworks inform how to meet them.

## Six design decisions

| Decision | What to settle | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| What learning matters here? | The course or unit obligation, its substantive concepts and skills, and connections to prior knowledge or later use that help students make meaning. | UbD desired results; QTM Intellectual Quality and Significance |
| What will reveal the learning? | An observable response, quality criteria, and the decision the evidence informs; reasoning where the target thinking requires it. | UbD evidence; QTM explicit quality; WWB assessment |
| How will students reach it? | Explanations, examples, guided attempts, practice and feedback chosen from actual readiness and budgeted against the sequence (`continuity-progression.md`). | WWB explicit teaching and feedback; UbD learning plan |
| How can students access the challenge? | The barrier, and support that removes it while keeping the target thinking with students (`scaffolding-differentiation.md`). | QTM high expectations; WWB; UDL |
| Can one teacher run it? | Visible next actions and quality; routines, resources, help and transitions (the walk-through in `workflow-lesson.md` §5). | QTM Quality Learning Environment; WWB classroom management |
| What changes next? | How learner work and feedback revise materials, support and sequence, and when students act on feedback. | WWB feedback and data; QTM reflection |

## Design review

Use for new or substantively changed lessons, units and assessment tasks; for a bounded edit, inspect the affected part and its dependencies.

1. **Set the purpose.** Name this artifact's role in the sequence (first explanation, fluency practice, consolidation, transfer or assessment) and judge it against that role.
2. **Read the actual draft:** the student questions, examples, instructions, expected responses and criteria. For a unit outline, the planned evidence sequence and any anchor tasks; mark missing task materials as unverified.
3. **Apply the three QTM lenses** to that evidence:
   - *Intellectual Quality:* which concept must the student use, and could a shortcut or copying complete the task without it? What response would separate understanding from the likely misconception?
   - *Quality Learning Environment:* can students see the first action and the expected quality, and is there a real chance to respond to feedback?
   - *Significance:* does each connection or context help meaning-making for these learners?
4. **Record each finding** as the exact question, slide or sequence entry, the mismatch with its purpose, and the repair. A design with no supported defect stays as it is.
5. **Repair within scope.** In create or adapt work, fix supported defects directly, preferring to replace weak work over appending more; synchronise answers, instructions and criteria. In review work, report.
6. **Recheck the changed path:** target thinking, support, time and downstream coverage. If a repair no longer fits the allocation, run the pacing steps in `workflow-unit-course.md`.

**Done when** every supported defect is repaired or reported with its fix, and the changed path still fits the time and coverage.

### Examples

- **Conceptual understanding:** a two-step-equation worksheet claims understanding but varies only the numbers. Replace one redundant item with an incorrect transformation for students to explain and correct; check the answer and time. A fluency lesson after established conceptual work keeps its repeated practice.
- **Supported entry:** a computing task assesses algorithm design but supplies the finished algorithm. Replace it with input and output examples or trace prompts, and confirm the support is permitted in formal assessment.
- **Locate the defect first:** before adding a group presentation or a story "for engagement", find the actual learning defect; if explanation is missing, turn an existing response into a brief justification and recalculate time.

## Framework audits on request

- **UbD:** desired results, then acceptable evidence, then the learning plan, mainly at unit and sequence level. A lesson inherits the alignment.
- **WWB 2025:** high expectations, explicit teaching, effective feedback, using data, assessment, classroom management, wellbeing and collaboration are interrelated practices; select those that bear on the current problem.
- **UDL:** anticipate barriers across engagement, representation, and action and expression; offer the options that serve the goal.
- **QTM:** Intellectual Quality, Quality Learning Environment and Significance. Document evidence covers 14 of the 18 elements; Engagement, Social Support, Students' Self-regulation and Inclusivity need observed enactment. For formal coding, follow the official guide's full procedure.

When sources disagree, compare their task, learners, phase, version and authority, and keep an unresolved disagreement visible. Provenance lives in SOURCES.md.

## APST

Use the career stage the request names; Graduate is the default for pre-service work. Write descriptors as targets (for example `targets 3.2.1`), as placement and university templates require. Whether a descriptor was demonstrated is the supervising teacher's or accreditor's judgement from documentary and practice evidence.

Map possible evidence conservatively, and keep its sources apart (plan or resource, observed teaching, student work, mentor feedback, reflection):

- Standards 1–2: students, diversity, disability, content and curriculum, literacy, numeracy and ICT.
- Standards 3–5: planning, teaching, resources, classroom environment, assessment, feedback, moderation, data and reporting.
- Standards 6–7: professional learning, collaboration, policy, ethics and community engagement, where genuinely present.

Primary source: [Australian Professional Standards for Teachers](https://www.aitsl.edu.au/docs/default-source/national-policy-framework/australian-professional-standards-for-teachers.pdf). AITSL was reviewing the Standards during 2026; recheck the current status before copying descriptors.
