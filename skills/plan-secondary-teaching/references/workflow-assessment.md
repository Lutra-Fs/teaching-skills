# Workflow: assessment, marking, feedback and evidence

Use for assessment schedules, task notifications, rubrics, marking and moderation, feedback, post-task review, and interpreting student work, progress or mentor feedback. Read `assessment-reporting.md` and the course card; `stage-and-pathway.md` for the reporting route; `scaffolding-differentiation.md` for permitted support; `hsc-exam-intelligence.md` only for actual exam material.

## Assessment tasks and marking

1. **Identify the phase and owner:** schedule, task, rubric, marking, feedback or review. Inherit the approved program and task, course and cohort, taught content, timeline and conditions. A formative question can go straight to design; a formal Stage 6 task needs its course rules confirmed before it is final.
2. **Separate authority:** mandatory NESA rules, official examples and school decisions. When a requirement is unresolved, look it up through `workflow-sources.md`, mark it unresolved, and keep drafting the other parts; weightings and conditions come only from the source.
3. **Do the phase:** for a schedule, map evidence opportunities and timing; for a task, map construct to observable evidence to criteria and conditions; for marking, apply verified guidance and look closely at borderline cases; for feedback, tie each comment to the evidence and to something the student can do next.
4. **Assess the core only:** taught teacher extensions are not assessed unless the task itself includes them.
5. **Check readiness:** students were taught and practised what is assessed before the task. If preparation needs course time, use the pacing steps in `workflow-unit-course.md`. Changes to a common task go to its owner as proposals.
6. **Check access and assistance** against the construct, and record assistance that affects what can be inferred about independence or authorship. Keep individual evidence inside group work. Life Skills and VET follow their own routes.
7. **Produce and check** the relevant modules of `assets/assessment-pack.md`: solve the questions, test for criterion overlap and alternative valid responses, and review the task with the material review in `frameworks-quality.md`. Student and teacher versions stay separate. **Done when** every question is solved, every criterion is distinct, and alternative valid responses are listed.

## Interpreting evidence

1. Work from anonymised evidence: student work, observation, scores, taught content, support given, mentor notes or a short reflection. Short notes are enough.
2. Separate what was observed from the interpretation. Distinguish scheduled, taught, practised and independently shown.
3. Compare with the criteria; look for a pattern and for counter-evidence. One attempt does not show stable ability. If the evidence is thin, state the limited conclusion and the smallest next check.
4. Choose the next action: keep or revise a resource, reteach a blocking prerequisite, target support, extend, gather more evidence, report, or adjust pace. Give a grade when the teacher asks for one.
5. Implementation goes to the right workflow: materials to `workflow-lesson.md`, timing to `workflow-unit-course.md`.

## Deliver

The requested product or audit with traceable criteria and checked conditions, or evidence, limits, interpretation, action and owner. Each conclusion is as strong as its evidence and no stronger; possible APST evidence comes from observed practice.
